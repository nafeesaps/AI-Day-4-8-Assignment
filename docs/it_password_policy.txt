IT and Password Policy

Employees must protect their company accounts and should never share their passwords with another person. Passwords must be unique to the company account and should not be reused for personal websites or services.

Employees should create strong passwords that are difficult to guess. A password should contain a combination of uppercase and lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters. Passwords should not contain easily available personal information such as a name, birthday, phone number, or company username.

Multi-factor authentication should be enabled whenever it is available for a company service. Employees must not approve unexpected authentication requests or share verification codes with anyone.

Company laptops should be locked whenever an employee leaves the device unattended. Employees should install approved security updates and should not disable company security software.

Suspicious emails, unexpected login notifications, or possible security incidents should be reported to the IT team as soon as possible. Employees should not open suspicious attachments or provide credentials through unknown links.

The IT team may reset an employee's password when there is evidence of a security concern. Employees should follow the IT team's instructions during security incidents and cooperate with required account recovery procedures.
